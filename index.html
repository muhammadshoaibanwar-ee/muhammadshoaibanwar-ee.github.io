Flagship Project 01  ·  Microprocessor Systems

Smart Home Security System, PIN-Protected Door Lock with Laser Latch

A three-state finite state machine on the ATmega328P handles idle, entry, and lockout. The keypad drives PIN entry, the I2C LCD gives real-time feedback, ascending PWM tones play on access granted and descending on denied, and a 60-second hard lockout triggers after three wrong attempts. The laser diode is the latch mechanism. Modelled in Tinkercad before being assembled and stress-tested across 50 consecutive entry cycles on breadboard hardware.

PlatformArduino Nano, ATmega328P at 16 MHz
Interface4x4 matrix keypad and 16x2 I2C LCD via PCF8574
LatchLaser diode, PWM-controlled, fail-secure on power-up
VerifiedTinkercad simulation plus 50-cycle physical test

Flagship Project 02  ·  Electronic Circuit Design

High-Precision Active Peak Detector, Diode Error Reduced to Under 10 µV

A standard diode peak detector loses around 700 mV to the forward drop before a measurement even starts. Enclosing the diode inside a TL082CD J-FET op-amp feedback loop reduces the effective error to under 10 µV. Accuracy holds within 3 percent from DC through 10 kHz. Designed and validated in NI Multisim at 0.02 percent simulation error, then physically soldered onto Vero board and cross-checked against a calibrated bench DMM. The Arduino ADC feeds filtered readings to a 16x2 LCD in real time.

Op-AmpTL082CD Dual J-FET at plus and minus 12 V supply
AccuracyUnder 3 percent error DC to 10 kHz, 0.02 percent in simulation
ReadoutArduino ADC 10-bit, moving-average filtered, to 16x2 LCD
HardwareFully soldered Vero board, validated against bench DMM
Analog DesignOp-AmpNI MultisimVero BoardSignal Processing
Flagship Project 03  ·  Electromagnetic Field Theory

Wireless Charging Tile for Smart City, First-Principles Electromagnetic Design

No simulation package was used for the core design. This coil was derived analytically using Biot-Savart Law and Faraday's Law from Ulaby 7th Edition. The derivation shows that exactly 32 transmitter turns deliver 5 A or more to a receiver coil at 5 cm separation, operating at 100 kHz on a flat Qi-compatible spiral. A custom Python model using NumPy and Matplotlib then generated three parameter-sweep graphs that independently confirmed the analytical result. This was the clearest demonstration yet that the theory taught in class actually produces real numbers.

Coil Design32-turn flat spiral, 8 cm radius, litz wire at 100 kHz
Output5.04 A delivered at 5 cm, meets Qi specification
TheoryBiot-Savart and Faraday's Law, derived analytically
ValidatedPython model with NumPy and Matplotlib, three parameter sweeps
